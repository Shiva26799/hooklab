## 1. What is Working
*Based on initial data from your top reel:*

* **Showing professional authority:** The top reel used a vet clinic setting and expert presence, scoring an engagement rate of **110.0**, compared to **28.0** for the text-only video.
* **Action-focused hooks:** Starting with a high-value checklist hook ("10 wellness tips that could add years to your dog’s life") clearly told viewers what they would gain right away.
* **Encouraging saves instead of link clicks:** The top-performing post asked users to "save" the post, while the weaker post asked users to click a link.
* **Broad wellness topics:** Focusing on general dog wellness tips performed better than focusing narrowly on general senior care without a clear angle.

---

## 2. What is Not Working
*Based on patterns in your lower-performing content:*

* **Text-only visuals:** Relying on plain text on screen without a person or professional setting lowered viewer engagement significantly.
* **Generic topic titles:** Vague hooks like "Guide to Senior Dog Care" lacked urgency and failed to give viewers a specific reason to stay.
* **Directing viewers off-platform:** Using a call to action that asks people to leave the app (like clicking a link) resulted in weaker engagement compared to asking for a save.

## 3. Five Video Ideas

### Idea 1: 3 Daily Habits to Help Your Dog Live Longer
* **Hook (0–2s):** "Here are three simple daily habits that could add years to your dog’s life."
* **Best Format:** Voiceover over footage of a clean vet clinic and dogs getting routine care, with text points on screen.
* **Script (30–40s):** 
  > "First, keep them lean. Carrying extra weight strains their joints and organ health. Second, brush their teeth daily. Gum disease feeds bacteria straight into the bloodstream. Third, add ten minutes of mental play every day. Brain activity keeps senior dogs sharp. Save this list so you can build these habits into your daily routine."

### Idea 2: The Truth About Your Dog’s Weight and Lifespan
* **Hook (0–2s):** "Keeping your dog lean can actually add up to two full years to their life."
* **Best Format:** Voiceover over video of a dog getting measured or weighed at a clinic, followed by healthy meal prep footage.
* **Script (30–40s):** 
  > "Most dogs carry a little extra weight, but even a couple of extra pounds put heavy stress on their heart and joints. You should easily feel your dog’s ribs without pressing hard. If you can’t, try trimming their meal portions by ten percent and swapping high-calorie treats for fresh cucumbers or carrots. Save this to check your dog's rib cage today."

### Idea 3: Why Dog Dental Care Matters More Than You Think
* **Hook (0–2s):** "Brushing your dog’s teeth isn’t just about breath—it protects their heart."
* **Best Format:** Voiceover over close-up footage of gentle dog dental care or a vet checking teeth.
* **Script (30–40s):** 
  > "Over eighty percent of dogs show signs of gum disease by age three. When gums get inflamed, bad bacteria enter the bloodstream and can damage the heart and kidneys over time. A quick brush with dog-safe toothpaste twice a week makes a massive difference. Save this video as a reminder to start a routine tonight."

### Idea 4: Why Food Variety Helps Your Dog's Gut
* **Hook (0–2s):** "Eating the exact same food every single day might not be best for your dog's gut."
* **Best Format:** Voiceover over footage of healthy food toppers (plain pumpkin, blueberries, fresh meat) being added to a dog bowl.
* **Script (30–40s):** 
  > "Just like us, dogs benefit from a diverse gut microbiome. Feeding the exact same bowl for years can limit good gut bacteria. You don't need to change their main kibble drastically, but adding small spoonfuls of safe whole foods—like plain canned pumpkin, cooked eggs, or blueberries—helps support healthy digestion. Save this to try at mealtime."

### Idea 5: 4 Subtle Signs Your Senior Dog Needs a Checkup
* **Hook (0–2s):** "If your dog is slowing down, don't just write it off as old age."
* **Best Format:** Voiceover over cozy footage of an older dog walking outside and resting comfortably at home.
* **Script (30–40s):** 
  > "Slowing down often means early stiffness or discomfort, not just aging. Watch out for hesitation before jumping on the couch, taking longer to stand up, slipping on floor tiles, or slight mood changes. Spotting these signs early lets your vet help manage joint health so your dog stays comfortable longer. Save this to track your senior dog's health."
