# Dog Longevity Content Report

## 1. What is working

* **Interactive DIY treats with health benefits:** Simple home recipes performed best by far. Video 1 (*"How old was your dog when you found out..."*) generated **5,060 likes and 5,538 comments**. Giving owners a clear recipe combined with a simple health angle drives massive viewer interaction.
* **Emotional stories featuring real senior dogs:** Sharing high-age milestones hits a strong emotional note. Video 2 (*"This is Chase. He's 18 and a half years old"*) brought in **2,670 likes and 92 comments** by focusing on a touching story about an aging dog.
* **Hope-filled recovery and mobility stories:** Comeback stories build strong audience hope. Video 3 (*"Never won the lottery, but six months ago my dog could barely walk"*) achieved **2,148 likes and 34 comments** by showing real physical recovery in an older dog.
* **Voiceover over footage format:** 100% of the top-performing reels used voiceover over dog footage. Pairing natural visual dog clips with clear storytelling drives better results than text-only or carousel formats.

---

## 2. What is not working

* **Vlog-style intros and calendar dates:** Starting videos with personal greetings like *"Happy Sunday, November 15th from The Golden Project"* failed to hold attention (**1 like, 0 comments**).
* **Abstract or theoretical topics:** Content focusing on high-level topics like "longevity pills" dominated the bottom group (67% of bottom reels). Video 2 in the weak group (*"Can we give our dogs more healthy years?"*) got only **1 like and 0 comments**. Viewers respond to immediate, practical care tips rather than general scientific concepts.
* **Casual videos without a clear takeaway:** Videos showing basic daily routines without a strong lesson or story—like *"Shower time with Baby"* (**14 likes, 0 comments**)—struggled to get engagement.
* **Weak or missing call-to-actions:** 67% of low-performing reels had no call-to-action or only linked out. Asking viewers a simple question leads to much higher engagement than leaving the video open-ended.

## 3. Five video ideas

### Idea 1: The Simple Joint-Care Smoothie
* **Title:** Homemade Joint Booster
* **Hook line (0–2s):** "If your senior dog is getting a little slow getting off the couch, try this."
* **Script (30–40s):**  
  "As dogs age, their joints get stiff, especially in the morning. Here is a simple two-ingredient topper I add to my dog's bowl twice a week. Take half a cup of plain bone broth with no added salt, and blend it with half a cup of fresh blueberries. Blueberries are packed with antioxidants, and real bone broth gives them collagen for joint support. Pour a small splash over their food. My dog licks the bowl clean every single time. How does your dog do on stairs lately?"
* **Best format:** Voiceover over dog footage (showing ingredients being prepped and dog eating).

### Idea 2: The 5-Second Rib Check
* **Title:** Is Your Dog at a Healthy Weight?
* **Hook line (0–2s):** "Keeping your dog light can actually add two whole years to their life."
* **Script (30–40s):**  
  "Extra weight puts constant pressure on a dog's heart and joint cartilage. But how do you know if your dog is actually at a good weight? Try the hand test. Gently run your fingers over their ribs. It should feel like running your fingers over the back of your hand—you can feel the ribs easily without pushing hard. If it feels like the palm of your hand, they might need to lose a pound or two. Try checking your dog today and tell me what you find."
* **Best format:** Voiceover over dog footage (demonstrating the rib check on a calm dog).

### Idea 3: Simple Habit for Cleaner Teeth
* **Title:** Dental Care Without the Fight
* **Hook line (0–2s):** "Bad breath in older dogs isn't just smelly—it affects their heart."
* **Script (30–40s):**  
  "Bacteria from gum disease can enter your dog's bloodstream and stress their internal organs over time. But if your dog hates toothbrushes, start small. Take a soft piece of gauze wrapped around your finger, dab a tiny bit of dog-safe toothpaste on it, and gently wipe just the outside of their side teeth for five seconds. You don't need to do the whole mouth on day one. Building the routine matters more than being perfect. How do you handle dental care at home?"
* **Best format:** Voiceover over dog footage (showing the gentle gauze technique).

### Idea 4: Muscle Maintenance Exercise
* **Title:** Low-Impact Mobility Move
* **Hook line (0–2s):** "Six months ago, getting up was tough for this old guy. Here is what helped."
* **Script (30–40s):**  
  "When older dogs lose muscle in their back legs, everyday movements get hard. One low-impact trick we use is slow step-ups. Find a wide, flat wooden board or low step just two inches off the ground. Lure your dog to put just their front paws up, hold for three seconds, and step down. Doing this five times builds hind leg strength without stressing their joints. Always check with your vet before starting new exercises. How is your dog's energy level these days?"
* **Best format:** Voiceover over dog footage (showing controlled, low-impact exercise).

### Idea 5: Daily Enriched Brain Game
* **Title:** Keep Senior Brains Sharp
* **Hook line (0–2s):** "Mental exercise wears out an older dog faster than a long walk."
* **Script (30–40s):**  
  "Keeping an aging dog's brain active is just as important as keeping their body moving. Take an old towel, scatter a few of their regular kibbles or low-calorie treats inside, roll it up tight, and tie it in a loose knot. Let your dog use their nose and paws to figure out how to unroll it. It builds confidence, reduces boredom, and keeps their brain working. Save this video to try it tonight."
* **Best format:** Voiceover over dog footage (showing towel setup and dog solving the puzzle).

## 4. Health claims to check before posting

To maintain trust as a health creator, review these claims seen in recent videos and ensure you cite proper sources or add clear disclaimers:

* **Claim:** *"Treat works like a natural antihistamine / calms itching and stops scratching"*
  * **What needs backing:** Specify *why* ingredients work (e.g., quercetin in apples, omega fatty acids). State clearly that homemade treats support comfort but do not replace prescribed allergy medication from a vet.
* **Claim:** *"Helps dogs live longer"*
  * **What needs backing:** Avoid direct guarantees. Frame this as supporting healthy aging or quality of life based on general veterinary wellness principles.
* **Claim:** *"Dog regained mobility after doing Pilates and physical therapy"*
  * **What needs backing:** Clarify that physical therapy results vary by condition and require guidance from a licensed veterinary rehabilitation specialist.
* **Claim:** *"Maintaining a healthy weight can add 2+ years to a dog's life"*
  * **What needs backing:** This is supported by veterinary research (such as the landmark 14-year Purina lifespan study), but state the general study reference so viewers know it is grounded in evidence.
* **Claim:** *"Brushing teeth prevents gum disease which is linked to major health issues"*
  * **What needs backing:** Supported by veterinary dentistry studies connecting periodontal disease to systemic inflammation affecting heart and kidney health. Mention that home brushing works best alongside professional dental exams.
* **Claim:** *"Diet rotation supports gut microbiome diversity"*
  * **What needs backing:** Mention that dietary changes should be made slowly to avoid gastrointestinal upset, especially in older dogs.

## 5. Honest limits

* **Small sample size:** Only 16 total reels were analyzed (13 with complete numbers). These results represent early signals rather than scientific proof.
* **Data limitations:** The data shows initial likes and comments, but does not provide exact view counts, completion rates, or long-term subscriber conversion. 
* **Variables outside our control:** Individual algorithm distribution, posting times, and audio trends can sway performance numbers independently of the video topic itself.

## 6. What could work next

1. **Test DIY health treats with simple questions:** Build on the success of Winner 1 (**5,060 likes, 5,538 comments**) by testing simple functional recipes (e.g., frozen joint bites, digestion toppers) paired with direct question hooks.
2. **Lead with real ages and comeback stories:** Use specific ages in titles and hooks (e.g., *"He's 14 years old and still loves his morning walk"*). Real age markers build instant trust and pull in owners of senior pets.
3. **Turn technical longevity concepts into simple daily habits:** Instead of discussing theoretical topics like "longevity pills," translate science into daily actions (e.g., weight checks, joint-safe exercises, dental routines).
4. **End videos with specific conversation starters:** Replace generic links or passive endings with direct questions about the viewer's dog (e.g., *"How old is your pup?"* or *"What trick works for your dog?"*) to maintain high comment counts.
